Technical Proposal

Overall Approach

We propose a tailored version of our established Story Studio programme, delivered as an intensive one-and-a-half-day workshop for cardiac surgeons at Edwards Lifesciences. Story Studio is highly interactive and practical, giving participants the tools and confidence to communicate clinical research in a structured and audience-centred way.

The programme combines clear, evidence-based communication principles with structured practice activities and constructive feedback in a supportive environment. By the end of the training, participants will have presented multiple times, received detailed individual and group feedback, and will leave with a proven framework and digital tools to continue developing their communication skills.

The training uses a number of different approaches, including Barbara Minto's Pyramid Principle: a structured methodology for organising ideas from the top down, so that the most important message leads, supported by evidence beneath. This is particularly effective for clinical and scientific presentations, where dense data can overwhelm even expert audiences. We also draw on the presentation design principles and data storytelling methodology of Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic, author of Storytelling with Data, to help participants present clinical data visually and clearly.

In our experience, when a scientific expert fails to connect with the audience, it is rarely the expertise of the speaker or a failure of the science that is the issue. It is the communication. Surgeons who present their research with clarity and human connection are the ones who get invited back, who build collaborations, and who move their field forward.

With the COMMENCE trial's seminal 10-year data now published, there is a real opportunity to help your emerging surgical leaders tell that story well: not just with data, but with the patient impact behind it.

Pre-Work (Distributed 2 weeks before)
  • Pre-course survey: Self-assessment of communication confidence, experience, and goals. This gives us baseline metrics for measuring improvement.
  • Lay abstract: Each participant writes a plain-language summary of their research or a dataset from the COMMENCE trial that they wish to present.
  • Slide preparation: Participants prepare 3-5 slides on their chosen topic (their own research or COMMENCE data). These will be used in workshop exercises.

Day One Sessions

Session 1: Language
The Building Blocks of Scientific Communication
Morning | 90 minutes

We begin with introductions and set the scene: this training is an opportunity to build a foundation for better communication generally. We then explore the three types of language that make communication memorable: visual (language that helps us see), emotional (language that helps us feel), and logical (language that helps us believe). This is particularly powerful for clinicians, who tend to default heavily to logical language at the expense of engagement.

  • Demonstration of how the balance of these language types transforms a clinical message
  • Quick exercises: Empty Photograph (visual storytelling from data), Emotion Mapping (identifying how clinical research connects to patient experience), and Three Pieces of Data (choosing just three data points to tell a compelling story)
  • Paired exercise: participants rewrite a section of their lay abstract using the language techniques and deliver it
  • Group discussion and feedback

Break: 15 minutes

Session 2: Story
Structuring Your Science
Mid-Morning | 90 minutes

Introduction of simple, repeatable story frameworks for research communication:

  • Setup-Conflict-Resolution (ideal for presenting new data that challenges existing practice)
  • Problem-Inspiration-Payoff (ideal for innovation and new therapies)
  • Past-Event-Present (ideal for presenting new directions, explaining how the clinical landscape has shifted and why a new approach is necessary)
  • Exploration of creative techniques, including "prisming" (presenting the same COMMENCE data from different stakeholder perspectives: surgeon, referring cardiologist, hospital administrator, patient), employing suspense, and using the "Opposites" storytelling device
  • Group activity: participants restructure a technical abstract into a clear narrative. In pairs, they deliver this three consecutive times, improving through constructive feedback

Day One Continued

Session 3: Presenting with Data
Slides, Structure, and the Pyramid Principle
Early Afternoon | 90 minutes

This session focuses on how to present clinical data in a way that is clear and appropriate for the audience. Many researchers overload slides with data, losing their audience before the key message lands.

  • The Pyramid Principle in practice: structuring a presentation from the conclusion down, not the methodology up
  • Slide design principles: what to show, what to say, and how to manage the gap between the two
  • The "behaviour change" framework: before building any presentation, define what you want your audience to think, feel, or do differently afterwards
  • Exercise: participants restructure their pre-prepared slides using the Pyramid Principle and present a 3-minute version to a small group
  • Facilitated feedback on clarity, structure, and audience connection

Break: 15 minutes

Session 4: On Camera and Delivery
Presence, Voice, and Performance
Late Afternoon | 75 minutes

This session covers the physical and vocal dimensions of presenting, including on-camera performance, a skill increasingly required as organisations record interviews, panel discussions, and promotional content with their clinical experts.

  • Confidence, presence, and non-verbal communication: what the research tells us about how audiences perceive speakers
  • Voice use: pace, pause, emphasis, and the power of silence
  • On-camera exercise: each participant delivers a 60-second summary of their key message to camera, followed by playback and individual feedback
  • Discussion: adapting your story to different formats (conference podium, panel discussion, internal meeting, recorded interview)
  • Reflection and preparation notes for Day 2

Session 5: Bringing It All Together
Final Presentations and Feedback
Morning | 150 minutes (plus 30 mins break) | Close: 12:30pm

The final session is where everything comes together. Each participant delivers a full 5-7 minute presentation incorporating all the techniques covered: structured narrative, effective data presentation, engaging language, and confident delivery.

  • Each participant presents to the full group
  • Structured peer feedback: every participant provides written observations using a feedback framework
  • Detailed individual feedback from the trainer on content, structure, delivery, and presence
  • On-camera recording of final presentations for personal review
  • Group reflection: key takeaways, personal action plans, and commitments

  • The Content Tool, a structured planning template for any future presentation or communication engagement
  • Course materials and reference guides from the workshop
  • 30-day email support: participants can reach out with specific queries related to an upcoming presentation or communication opportunity
